Mifepristone REMS Program — Overview

What is REMS?
Mifepristone is subject to a Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) — an FDA safety program that requires prescribers to sign a prescriber agreement form and be REMS-certified before they can prescribe the drug. REMS stands for Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy.

The REMS applies to both uses of mifepristone: early pregnancy loss (EPL) management and medication abortion. One enrollment covers both indications.

There are two FDA-approved mifepristone products, each with its own REMS enrollment portal:
- Mifeprex (brand name, manufactured by Danco Laboratories)
- Generic mifepristone (manufactured by GenBioPro)

REMS Requirements for Prescribers
Clinicians who want to prescribe mifepristone must complete a prescriber agreement form before ordering or dispensing mifepristone. This is a one-time enrollment — certification does not expire. The prescriber agreement form is available directly from each manufacturer.

REMS Requirements for Patients
Patients must sign a patient agreement form (also called the Medication Guide acknowledgment) before receiving mifepristone. This form must be signed and included in the discharge paperwork.

REMS Requirements for Pharmacies
Pharmacies must be separately certified under the REMS program to dispense mifepristone. Not all pharmacies carry mifepristone — prescribers should confirm availability before sending a prescription.

Why Does the REMS Exist?
The FDA implemented the REMS because of rare but serious risks associated with mifepristone, including serious infection and heavy bleeding. The REMS is designed to ensure that patients are informed of these risks and that appropriate follow-up is in place.

ACOG Position
The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists advocates the removal of REMS restrictions for mifepristone, stating that the restrictions create unnecessary barriers to clinician and patient access to medication abortion and EPL management. (ACOG Practice Bulletin No. 225, 2020)
